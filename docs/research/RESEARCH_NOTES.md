# Research notes: ideas to adopt in our own way

How this file works: the owner sends screenshots and notes from other projects' documentation. Every idea goes here, with its source, whether it fits our rules, and a status. **Nothing here is acted on until the owner says "consolidate findings".** Ideas are adopted by re-implementing them on our own permissive stack, never by copying code or adding a restricted dependency.

Our rules that every idea is checked against: permissive open-source licences only; no per-document vendor fee; no document or personal data leaves our infrastructure; Node 24 backend; deterministic output (parser version, frozen outputs).

Status values: `noted` (not looked at further), `fits` (compatible, worth building), `partly` (take the idea, not the tool), `rejected` (breaks a rule).

## 1. PyMuPDF documentation (Artifex), noted 2026-10-21

**Tool itself: rejected.** PyMuPDF is AGPL, with a commercial licence sold by Artifex Software. AGPL can require publishing our server code when users reach it over a network; the paid licence breaks the no-licence-fee rule. It is also a Python library and our backend is Node. Take the ideas only.

## 9. OCR measurements, 2026-10-22 (scan and photo sets, generated CVs; not real documents)

A usable gate needs different signals (for example word-level agreement between two reads, or detecting known-skill hits against the answer set).

## 10. Many layouts, one job (a conversation with someone who solved this for retail till slips, 2026-10-22)

Different branches printed different slip formats, so no single reading worked; he wrote a script that recognises each format and handles it. For RoleMatch the same idea is two steps: (1) recognise the layout family, (2) read it with rules suited to that family. Step 1 is built as a recorded label (`src/templates.ts`, stored as `meta.layoutFamily` with its evidence; reading is unchanged). The point the research misses: when Tesseract fails, it produces visible nonsense that our reader detects and flags (`ocrQuality.ts`). When a vision-language OCR model fails, it can write fluent text that is not on the page, and the evidence check ("the quote must be in the document") cannot catch it, because for a scan the document text *is* the OCR output. So a vision OCR model may only be used with a second, independent reader (Tesseract or classic PaddleOCR) and with disagreements flagged for a person. Most CVs are typed PDF or Word files that need no OCR at all; OCR matters for scans and photos. Owner instruction: **nothing is built for Tesseract or OCR until a trial on real scans** (ML_ARCHITECTURE phase 2, behind the existing `OcrEngine` interface). Status: logged, not built.

**PaddleOCR trial, first result (2026-10-22, one generated CV, `OCR_ENGINE=paddle npm run eval:ocr -- --quick`).** The engine is in `server/src/ocr.ts` behind `OcrEngine`, off by default (MIT wrapper `@gutenye/ocr-node` running PP-OCRv4 through ONNX Runtime, in memory, no temporary files). Words recovered (F1): clean scan Tesseract 99% vs Paddle 27%; poor phone photo 93% vs 77%. Cause of the clean-scan gap: the bundled recognition model is the Chinese-and-English one, whose dictionary has no space character, so it reads the letters correctly but runs words together ("Perioperativenursewithsevenyears"). Names, numbers and punctuation came out right, and it is fast (about 1 s a page). A character-level comparison I tried afterwards was badly aligned and is not a result. **Not a verdict on PaddleOCR**, only on this model file: the fair test needs an English or Latin-script recognition model (Latin covers Afrikaans accents), which is a separate download. Tesseract stays the live engine.

**PaddleOCR trial, full comparison (2026-10-22; generated scans and photos, 7 CVs of 1 to 4 pages, 6 degradation profiles; not real documents).** Setup: `OCR_ENGINE=paddle OCR_PADDLE_DIR=server/data/ocr-models`, the bundled PP-OCRv4 line detector plus the English recognition model `en_PP-OCRv3_rec_infer.onnx` (8,967,018 bytes, sha256 `ef7abd8b…`, from huggingface.co/SWHL/RapidOCR, Apache-2.0) and `en_dict.txt` (190 bytes, PaddleOCR, Apache-2.0). Words recovered, Tesseract versus Paddle: clean scan 99% / 96%, skewed 100% / 96%, faded 99% / 88%, phone good 100% / 94%, phone poor 91% / 71%, worst case 0% / 4% (both unreadable). Names, emails, phones, companies and skills were also worse with Paddle (for example companies 33% against 100% on skewed and phone-good images; email 67% on a faded scan). Speed is the same (about 1 to 3 s a page on this Mac). Paddle reports 95 to 97% confidence even where it recovered 71% of the words, so its own confidence cannot be used as a quality signal. Two things it does better: it read a name Tesseract mangled ("Dlamini" against "Diamini"), and its errors fall in different places from Tesseract's, which makes it a candidate **second reader** for emails and phone numbers (read both, flag disagreement). **Verdict: not better than Tesseract on this data, so Tesseract stays the live engine.** Not tested: Afrikaans accents (the English model has no accented letters), real scans, tables. Setup traps found: the wrapper appends a space to the character list itself, so the list's final newline must be removed but its own space entry kept (done in `ocr.ts`); the bundled Chinese-and-English recognition model drops all spaces. Next if pursued: a Latin-script model, the second-reader idea, and real scans.

**PaddleOCR trial, second round: a fair test (2026-10-22).** The owner pointed out that the first comparison was unfair: Tesseract had weeks of tuning and Paddle had none. Correct. Everything engine-independent (image preparation, column logic, e-mail and lookalike repairs, quality gates) was already shared, but four things were tuned for Tesseract only or were wrong for Paddle, and were fixed in `server/src/paddleOcr.ts` and `ocr.ts`:
1. **Input size.** The shared preparation delivers about 3000 px pages (right for Tesseract); the line detector expects about 1000 to 1500. Pages are scaled to 1280 (`OCR_PADDLE_LONG_EDGE`).
2. **The wrapper departs from PaddleOCR's own settings** (detector threshold 0.03 instead of 0.3; no ImageNet mean and deviation on the detector input; 0..1 instead of (x/255 - 0.5)/0.5 on the recognition input). The detector and recognition steps now run with PaddleOCR's published values.
3. **Words with positions.** The wrapper returns whole lines, so the column logic could not separate two columns (one box spanned both). The recognition network marks each letter at one horizontal step, so words now come back with real positions; a long empty run splits a line (a gutter or table cell); a space the network itself read keeps two words together; the letter pitch of each line gives word extents. Boxes are brought back to ink height (the detector's boxes are about 1.6 times taller than the letters, the median of 18 pages).
4. **Paddle's own error patterns.** A ruled vertical line between columns is read as a column of bars and is dropped when the bars line up on four or more rows; a letter straight after a comma gets its space (the English model rarely emits one).
**Result.** Character error on the 38-CV tuning sample (`stress:ocrtune`, the protocol used to tune Tesseract; lower is better): scans Tesseract 21.4% against Paddle 14.3%; photos 18.5% against 13.9%. Paddle is clearly ahead on dark sidebars, coloured headings and phone photos of multi-column pages. On the 7 sample CVs with field checks (`eval:ocr`) they are level on clean scans, skewed scans and good phone photos (words 94 to 95% against 99 to 100%, same names, e-mails, roles and companies), and Tesseract is still ahead on faded copies and poor photos (words 99% against 88%, 91% against 80%; companies 100% against 22%). On the worst-case images Paddle recovers a little (5% of words, some names) and reports 49% confidence, where Tesseract recovers nothing. **Verdict: neither engine wins everywhere, so the next step is not a swap but a choice per document**: read with both, score each reading with our own dictionary-free plausibility check (`ocrQuality.ts`), keep the better one, and flag disagreement on e-mails and phone numbers for a person. Not built yet. Still untested: Afrikaans accents (English model), real scans, tables, handwriting. Set-up: `OCR_ENGINE=paddle OCR_PADDLE_DIR=server/data/ocr-models` (the two model files are git-ignored; their source and hashes are above). Defaults are PaddleOCR's own; every setting is an `OCR_PADDLE_*` variable for measuring only.
