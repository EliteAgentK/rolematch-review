RoleMatch programme plan

How the LLM becomes part of the system, how the 5,000 and 30,000 user tests run, and how each piece is proven. Written 5 November 2026, about 09:30 SAST; updated about 12:00 with your auditor rule and what long CVs have shown. Report due about 20:00.

What changes, in one paragraph

The LLM runs as a background worker, the chef in the back. A record is published by the rules straight away. Minutes or seconds later the model reads the same document, and everything it proposes must quote the document. Configuration then decides, per field and document type, whether a proposal improves the record on its own, goes to a recruiter, or is only logged. A kind of proposal earns automatic use only by being measured accurate. Every recruiter answer feeds a private store that the next prompt or adapter is tested against. Scores stay arithmetic: the model never sets a percentage.

Your new rule: the LLM audits every CV before the panel

This replaces "publish first, read afterwards" for CVs. Nothing loads into a panel until the auditor has passed it. Where the parser struggled, the auditor corrects the record, and every correction must quote the CV. The rules still calculate every percentage.

Your decisions, now in the plan

  • Training: all CVs and job descriptions in the Mac's Downloads folder. Yours to answer for on consent; kept on this machine.
  • Job titles: earn credit only where relevant to the specific job.
  • The model: it learns from every test and is given a brief on the whole system.
  • Backups: left alone for now. Efficiency is the concern.
  • Big tests first: 30,000, then work down.

What this plan will not claim

  • That the LLM is accurate before its table says so.
  • That one laptop GPU serves a large platform. The design scales; this hardware does not.
  • That 30,000 test sessions equal 30,000 real people on separate networks.
  • That nothing is weak. Weak areas go in the report with their figures.
