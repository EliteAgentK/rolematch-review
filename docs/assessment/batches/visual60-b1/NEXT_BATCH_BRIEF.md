# Next batch brief (after visual60-b1)

This brief asks for a **small, carefully labelled** batch, not another large one. Format variants and copies do not count as new evidence. The next batch should help tell apart:

* fixes that generalise to unseen designs;
* fixes that only fit this pack's 60 designs;
* the parser's real weaknesses, as opposed to artefacts of simulation.

## 1. Unresolved failure classes to target (development portion)

| Class | What to include | Positive, negative and boundary examples |
|---|---|---|
| Name over two lines without a matching e-mail | 6 CVs whose e-mail does not contain the name (for example info@, initials only) | headings stacked like names ("PROFILE" / "SUMMARY") as negatives |
| Job title under a decorative folio or banner | 6 jobs | a folio that *is* the title; a title that wraps onto two lines |
| Employer next to an e-mail or website in the job header | 6 jobs | a company name containing "careers" |
| Application information next to requirements | 6 jobs | an "Application requirements" heading that *is* a real requirement ("valid driver's licence") |
| Certification lines (name, issuer, status, year) | 8 CVs | an in-progress certification; one with no issuer; one with a date range |
| Candidate location | 8 CVs | a place inside a line of contact details; "Remote"; two places |
| Two-column reading order on photographed pages | 6 CVs | a single column; three columns |

## 2. A broad sample to detect unrelated regressions

20 CVs and 10 jobs from **new** career content, **not adapted from the earlier corpus**. These should use ordinary layouts, so improvements on the targeted classes can be checked for side effects. Each in Word and native PDF only, unless OCR is in scope.

## 3. Novel challenge cases, never used to choose a fix (protected)

* **CVs (12):**
  * designs not seen in any earlier pack;
  * one long CV of 3 to 4 pages;
  * one very sparse CV;
  * one with contradictory dates;
  * one with instructions embedded in hidden or visible text, as an adversarial case;
  * one with a career break;
  * one with concurrent part-time roles.
* **Jobs (6):**
  * new job families;
  * nested alternatives ("degree in A or B, or 5 years of C and D");
  * one job with no essential section.
* **Allocation.** These go to the protected portion before anyone looks at results.

## 4. Document conditions

* **Real captures, if possible.** A few genuine phone photographs and genuine scans, of fictional documents, printed and captured with consent. Deterministic simulations cannot show real-camera performance.
* **Page lengths.** 1 to 4 pages.
* **Languages.** English only, per the owner's rule.

## 5. Labels and evidence each record needs

* **Extraction.** Field values exactly as printed, with:
  * the page and an approximate position for each value;
  * the role each duty belongs to;
  * completion status as printed.

  Generator source records and printed values must be distinguished explicitly.
* **Matching.** For about 10 candidate–job pairs, criterion-level labels made by **two people independently**, with disagreements adjudicated under the written rubric (`docs/assessment/PILOT_PROTOCOL.md`, section 2). Each label records:
  * the state (demonstrated, partial, transferable, no evidence, unreadable, ambiguous, contradicted, documented shortfall);
  * the supporting quote.

  The labels must not be generated by the same model or system that is evaluated. Without them, matching accuracy cannot be measured.
* **Formula B (optional).** A few explicitly labelled **fictional preference scenarios**: confirmed needs and offer terms, kept separate from CV content.

## 6. Lineage and duplicates

* Every record's lineage is stated: whether it is new content or adapted, and from which earlier record.
* Variants of one record carry one id.
* The pack is checked with `server/test/batch/inventory.ts` before use.

## 7. What a good result looks like

* **Targeted classes** improve on their own examples.
* **The broad sample** shows no right-to-wrong regressions.
* **The protected challenge cases** show, honestly, how well the fixes generalise; it is acceptable for them not to.
