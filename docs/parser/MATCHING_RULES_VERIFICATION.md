# Matching rules: what each judgement rests on

Generated by `npm run eval:judgements` from rules 2.4.0 (fingerprint `81ada17159ec`), assessment date 2026-11-04. Invented people. Enforced by `server/test/match-judgements.test.ts`.

## What this shows and what it does not

- **Shows:** each rule does what it says, on a case where it should fire and on a case where it must not. Every figure below is the matcher's own output.
- **Does not show:** that the rules are good at judging people. These expected answers were reasoned out by the author of the rules. **No recruiter has judged any of them, and no hiring outcome has been compared.** Matching quality is unvalidated.
- **Ordering tests are a different thing.** On the 60 invented pairs, same-role candidates rank above adjacent-role ones. That shows the score moves the right way on documents written to differ. It is not evidence that a given score is right.
- **These matching rules are not behind the pipeline switch.** They apply to every new match wherever this code runs, and each result records its rules version.

How a contribution is worked out: a requirement's credit (0, 0.5 or 1 under the current settings) times its weight (mandatory 10, preferred 4), divided by the total weight of the job's requirements. With one requirement, as in most cases below, that requirement is the whole score. A mandatory requirement contradicted by the CV also caps the result at 40.
